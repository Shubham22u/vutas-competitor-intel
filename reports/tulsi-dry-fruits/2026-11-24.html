Competitor Intel · Day 16 · 2nd look

Tulsi Dry Fruits

Researched November 24, 2026 · previous report: Oct 7, 2026

This is a follow-up deep-dive, not a repeat. The Oct 7 report already covered the founding story (Balbeer Bajaj, KBB Nuts, 1996 processing pivot, 2013 Gourmia launch), the ₹700 Cr (2019) revenue figure, and the core channel map in detail — see that report for the full history. This pass focuses on what's changed or newly surfaced since then: a corporate-address correction, a much fuller retail-channel list pulled directly off Gourmia's own site, fresh November 2026 pricing, a live execution problem on Tulsi's own D2C site, and — the most important new finding — hard industry data showing quick-commerce platforms are now racing into exactly the premium/gourmet dry-fruits niche Gourmia occupies, and Gourmia is not yet in that race.

Correction & clarification: HQ vs. manufacturing root

The origin story (Amritsar, Balbeer Bajaj, KBB Nuts) stands. But both brand storefronts list their operating addresses in New Delhi, not Amritsar: Tulsi's own-site contact is KBB Nuts Pvt. Ltd., Rampura Industrial Area, New Delhi, and Gourmia's is SKB International, B-57, Lawrence Road Industrial Area, New Delhi-110035 (both route customer email to the same eshop@bajajfoodgroup.com). Read together with the Oct 7 finding that processing plants sit in Delhi, Punjab (Amritsar), Haryana and Andhra Pradesh, the picture is: Amritsar is the founding/manufacturing root, but the group's commercial/e-commerce operations are run out of Delhi NCR. Worth noting precisely because VUTAS's own "About" story should be similarly precise about founding place vs. operating base to avoid the same kind of ambiguity flagged in the last report.

Where they sell — new detail: the full retail-channel list

Gourmia's own homepage runs an "Available on Multiple Platforms" banner strip that is considerably more complete than what surfaced via search in October. The full list now confirmed directly from the brand: Amazon, Flipkart, Flipkart Wholesale, Meesho, Snapdeal, JioMart, Udaan, Metro Cash & Carry, More Mega Store, More Supermarket, Spar Hypermarket, Tesco, Spencer's, Doranjee, Fair Shop, and Barista.

Three of these are genuinely new information versus the Oct 7 report: Udaan (India's largest B2B wholesale marketplace — confirms Gourmia/Tulsi also sell wholesale digitally to kirana/retailers, not just through offline distributors), Flipkart Wholesale (same logic, Flipkart's B2B arm), and Barista (the café chain — suggests Gourmia nuts are stocked as a grab-and-go snack/gifting item in a HoReCa-adjacent channel, a distribution angle no other brand in this research series has shown). Snapdeal, More (both store formats) and Doranjee/Fair Shop (smaller regional/online grocers) round out a channel list that is wider and more opportunistic than the "big 4 marketplaces + big 4 hypermarkets" picture the last report painted.

Still confirmed absent: Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart. No quick-commerce listing appears anywhere on either brand's own channel disclosure, six weeks on from the last check — see the industry-context finding below for why that gap is now more consequential than it looked in October.

Execution check: a live problem on tulsidryfruits.com

Direct inspection of the Tulsi homepage in November 2026 found injected third-party spam content mixed into the page body — unrelated links to replica-watch sellers, vape shops, phone-accessory sites and a smartwatch-strap site. This reads as a compromised or poorly maintained WordPress/plugin stack, not intentional content. It's a concrete, visible version of the "thin digital brand story" gap flagged in October — a ₹700 Cr+ legacy FMCG house is running a consumer storefront that currently serves spam to visitors alongside its almond listings.

Homepage pricing was still readable despite this: Tulsi California Rozana Almonds 1kg ₹1,449 (MRP ₹1,845), 500g ₹749 (MRP ₹940), 250g ₹399 (MRP ₹470); Almond Kernel Rozana Value 1kg ₹1,299 (MRP ₹1,765); Almond Kernel California 1kg ₹1,499 (MRP ₹1,915); Shahi Dates Safawi 500g ₹495. A Badam Giri Mamra Zaika 500g pack (₹1,898, MRP ₹2,450) was out of stock.

Industry context that changes the read on this company

Two pieces of market data surfaced this round reframe Tulsi/Gourmia's position rather than just adding detail to it:

The category is still mostly unbranded. India's dry fruits & nuts market is roughly $9.3B / ₹77,000 Cr (2024), projected to reach $12.7B by 2029 at a 6.5% CAGR — but an estimated ~70% of all sales are still loose and unbranded, worth roughly ₹50,000–60,000 Cr of the total. Branded players — Tulsi/Gourmia included — are still a minority of the category by value, competing against kirana-store loose bins that are typically 15–30% cheaper. India imports the large majority of its nuts (the US, Middle East, Turkey and Ghana account for ~80% of consumption; $2.85B imported in 2023 versus only 50,000–60,000 tonnes of domestic output), so currency and import-duty moves are a real structural risk for every branded player, Tulsi included.

Quick commerce is actively moving into premium/gourmet dry fruits right now — and Gourmia isn't there yet. As of mid-2026: Blinkit is piloting a "Gourmet" format from ~5 dark stores in Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR and Mumbai (artisanal/imported goods); Zepto is launching "Select," an imported/premium tab; Instamart has added a premium section; and Flipkart Minutes was reported to be readying its own gourmet push with a private label ("Pykd"). The stated logic from industry analysts: once every platform delivers in ~10 minutes, speed stops being a differentiator, so platforms are chasing premium-basket margin instead — premium baskets run ₹1,000–1,500 AOV versus ₹500–620 for staples runs, and Blinkit's own adjusted EBITDA (~₹37 Cr last quarter) is already leaning on premium-basket contribution. Specialist curators like FirstClub and Handpickd are reportedly seeing ~70% repeat-purchase rates in this lane, though one analyst (Satish Meena, Datum Intelligence) cautions the addressable luxury-basket market is still a thin slice confined to the top 8 cities, not a mass opportunity.

Put together: Gourmia is positioned exactly where this new platform-driven demand is forming — premium, flavoured, gift-worthy nuts — yet it has no quick-commerce listing as of this check, while generalist platforms are actively building private-label and curated competition into that same shelf space. That's a live, time-sensitive gap, not a settled one.

What VUTAS can take from this (update)

  • The quick-commerce premium shelf war is happening now, and this is the moment to get in, not after it crowds. If a ₹700 Cr+ player with a dedicated premium brand (Gourmia) still isn't on Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart, the premium dry-fruits shelf on those apps is still open — but not for long, given Blinkit's own "Gourmet" pilot and Flipkart's private-label "Pykd" are already moving. VUTAS should treat securing early placement in these platforms' emerging premium sections as a near-term priority once the new site is live, rather than a someday-channel.
  • But size the opportunity honestly. The analyst caution that luxury-basket demand is a thin slice of the top 8 cities matters for VUTAS's planning — this is a high-margin, low-volume lane worth being early in, not a channel to bet the whole go-to-market on. Treat it as one strong acquisition/margin channel alongside D2C, not a replacement for building the core site.
  • Site reliability and security are a brand asset, not just plumbing. Seeing a legacy competitor's own storefront serving spam content is a concrete argument for VUTAS to budget real attention (dependency hygiene, monitoring, a hardened CMS/headless setup) into the Next.js/Vercel rebuild — a clean, fast, trustworthy site is itself a competitive edge against incumbents who haven't kept theirs up.
  • The 70%-unbranded, import-dependent category context is useful positioning ammunition. VUTAS's pitch isn't just "nicer than loose bazaar nuts" — it's traceable sourcing and consistent quality in a category where the large majority of volume is still unbranded and importers are exposed to currency/duty swings. That's a real trust story worth stating plainly on the new site, not just implying through packaging.
  • Unconventional channels are worth a scan, cheaply. Gourmia's presence on Udaan (B2B wholesale), Flipkart Wholesale, and a café chain (Barista) shows a premium dry-fruits brand can pick up incremental, low-effort distribution outside the obvious D2C + Amazon + quick-commerce set. Worth a lightweight exploration later — e.g. a café or hotel gifting tie-up — once VUTAS's core channels are humming, rather than a near-term priority.
  • Keep watching this one. Tulsi/Gourmia is the only brand in this rotation that is both large-scale and visibly slow-moving in digital/quick-commerce — which makes it the most useful "what happens if a big legacy player stays complacent" case study to re-check periodically, specifically for whether it finally shows up on Blinkit/Zepto.
