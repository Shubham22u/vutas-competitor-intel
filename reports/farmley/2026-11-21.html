Competitor Intel · Day 13 · Refresh #2

Farmley

Researched November 21, 2026 — first covered October 4, 2026
Biggest development since the last report: in mid-October 2026, media reports said FMCG majors (Emami, ITC, Britannia named; Orkla India named then denied) were circling a stake in Farmley at a potential ₹2,000 Cr valuation, with ~31% of the cap table (early VCs) reportedly up for sale. Status as of this report: unconfirmed/denied by at least one named party — treat as live speculation, not a closed deal. Full detail in "Funding" below.

Origin story

Founded in July 2017 by Akash Sharma (CEO, IIT Delhi) and Abhishek Agarwal (IIT Roorkee), two engineering graduates who were flatmates exploring business ideas together. Rather than launching a packaged snack brand on day one, they went upstream first: roughly four years building backend/supply-chain infrastructure — direct relationships with farming communities and farm-gate processing capability — before putting real marketing weight behind the consumer brand.

The gap they identified wasn't price or flavour, it was adulteration and traceability in a dry fruits & nuts market that is roughly 95% unorganized/loose-trade. Their answer was "full-stack" vertical integration: own the sourcing (5,000+ farmers and producers) and the processing (five farm-gate processing units near origin) rather than buying finished nuts from traders and repackaging, which is how most D2C nut brands operate.

Headquartered in Noida (Delhi NCR). A $2M seed round in 2020 funded the brand-facing push; real commercial traction (the founders cite 10x revenue growth) came largely in the ~18 months immediately before their 2022 Series A — the backend-first bet took years to pay off, then compounded quickly once it did.

FY25 cost detail: total expenditure ₹419 Cr (up 62% from ₹257.2 Cr) — cost of materials ₹280.8 Cr (67% of expenses, up 53%), employee costs ₹27.2 Cr (up 71%), and marketing/advertising spend ₹51.8 Cr, which nearly doubled (up 96%) and now runs at roughly 13% of revenue. Expense growth is outpacing the loss-narrowing story — Farmley is still buying growth hard, not coasting to profitability. Revenue is 99.6% domestic (₹393 Cr); exports were just ₹70.13 lakh — a rounding error against the brand's publicized US/Middle East/Australia international push. CEO Akash Sharma stated in mid-2025 that Farmley was targeting ₹600–700 Cr revenue and profitability in FY26, funded partly by a planned ₹40–50 Cr new factory near Noida.

Funding history: $2M seed (2020) → $6M Series A (Aug 2022, DSG Consumer Partners & Alkemi Growth Capital, with Omnivore, Insitor Partners, angels) → $6.7M Pre-Series B (BC Jindal Group lead, DSG/Omnivore/Alkemi follow-on) → $40–42M Series C (May 2025, led by L Catterton, with DSG and BC Jindal Group). ~70% of Series C was primary capital, ~30% secondary — Insitor Partners and Samunnati fully exited then.

October 2026 development: media reports (Agro & Food Processing, StartupTalky, others) said a group of early investors holding a combined 31% stake — named as Omnivore Partners, DSG Consumer Partners, Alkemi Growth Capital, Insitor Partners, Indigram Labs and Trifecta Capital — had put their shares up for sale, with FMCG majors Emami, ITC, Britannia and Orkla India named as potential buyers at a deal that could value Farmley around ₹2,000 Cr. L Catterton (~21% stake) was reported unlikely to sell; founders (~32% combined) had not disclosed intent. KPMG was reportedly advising the sellers, with preliminary bids expected by end of October 2026. By Orkla India's own statement (reported October 12, 2026), the company denied any active proposal. No confirmed outcome or signed deal has been reported as of this write-up — this should be read as an active rumor/process, not a completed transaction.

Where they sell

D2C: farmley.com  ·  Marketplaces: Amazon, Flipkart  ·  Quick commerce: Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, plus BigBasket — now a four-app quick-commerce footprint, up from the two apps (Blinkit, Zepto) noted in October's report.

Offline: 15,000+ retail counters confirmed as of the FY25 financial filing, against a long-stated target of 1,00,000+ outlets — still a general-trade-heavy ambition with no publicized owned/flagship retail stores (unlike Happilo).

International: publicized early-stage presence in the US, Middle East and Australia, but FY25 filings show export revenue of only ₹70.13 lakh against ₹393 Cr domestic — i.e., essentially no real revenue from the international footprint yet, whatever the press narrative suggests.

Ownership/structure in flux: see the funding section — a meaningful slice of the cap table was reportedly for sale to strategic FMCG buyers as of October 2026.

Notable shift from the October report: plain almonds and cashews — Farmley's historical top sellers — no longer appear on the site's own bestsellers page at all. The storefront's hero real estate is now entirely Date Bites, Panchmeva mixes and seeds (pumpkin, chia, basil), plus a flavoured Roasted Makhana line (Peri Peri, Cheesy Cheddar, Minty Pudina, Salt & Pepper, Tangy Tomato under the "Makha Shaka" sub-brand) — several flavours of which were sold out at the time of this check, suggesting demand is outrunning supply on the newer SKUs. Plain nuts are still sold (e.g. via Amazon/BigBasket/Flipkart) but have clearly been demoted on Farmley's own D2C storefront in favour of higher-margin, harder-to-commoditize branded formats. Standing site-wide offers: 8% off above ₹899, 10% off above ₹1,500, free shipping above ₹5,000, and 10% off the first order for email signups.

Strategy, positioning & product range

Farmley's own homepage copy has shifted from the earlier "full-stack snacking brand" framing toward softer lifestyle language — "reimagining snacking as a delightfully purposeful part of everyday life" — while the underlying four-pillar story (trust, better-for-you nutrition, taste, innovation) and the adulteration-free/farm-to-pack traceability pitch remain the brand's foundation.

Marketing spend nearly doubled YoY (₹51.8 Cr, +96%) in FY25, explicitly described in filings as stepped-up "brand building" and "customer acquisition." That's being spent to push the innovation SKUs (Date Bites, flavoured makhana, seed mixes) rather than defend share in commodity almonds/cashews, where margin is thin and differentiation is hard. The emergence of serious FMCG acquisition interest (Emami/ITC/Britannia reportedly circling, even with Orkla's denial) signals that large strategics now see India's branded healthy-snacking category — Farmley specifically — as a credible roll-up/consolidation target, not just a VC-funded niche.

What VUTAS can take from this

  • Lead with an ownable format, not a commodity, on the new homepage. Farmley has quietly removed plain almonds/cashews from its own bestsellers page entirely, replacing them with Date Bites, flavoured makhana and seed mixes — categories nobody else sells under that exact name. For the Next.js rebuild, VUTAS's hero SKU on the homepage and first checkout step should be a signature curated item (a specific anjeer preparation, a house trail mix, a named combo) rather than "Almonds" or "Cashews" as the lead product — commodity nuts are a weak differentiator even for a brand with Farmley's scale and backend investment.
  • Marketing spend outpacing revenue growth is a warning, not a playbook to copy. Farmley's ad spend grew 96% against 71% revenue growth, and net loss only narrowed 15% despite that — at ₹394 Cr scale, customer acquisition is still expensive and margin-negative in this category. VUTAS, bootstrapped and far smaller, should resist the temptation to "spend into growth" the way funded competitors do; a lower, disciplined CAC with the curated catalog's naturally higher margin is a structural advantage worth protecting, not a sign of being behind.
  • "International expansion" claims in press coverage are mostly aspirational — don't let that distort VUTAS's own roadmap. Despite years of publicized US/Middle East/Australia presence, Farmley's actual FY25 export revenue was ₹70 lakh against ₹393 Cr domestic — essentially nothing. VUTAS should treat India-first D2C plus Indian quick commerce as the real near-term opportunity and not over-invest in export infrastructure or messaging before domestic repeat-purchase is proven.
  • Sold-out flavour SKUs are an inventory-planning lesson, not just a demand signal. Several of Farmley's five Roasted Makhana flavours were out of stock on its own site. If VUTAS launches flavoured or limited variants of its curated catalog, it should plan supply buffers before pushing acquisition spend on a new SKU — a stockout right after a marketing push wastes exactly the paid traffic it's trying to convert.
  • Farmley's cart-level offer structure is a directly reusable template. Tiered discounts by spend threshold (8% off ₹899+, 10% off ₹1,500+), free shipping above ₹5,000, and a 10%-off email-capture incentive is a simple, proven AOV-and-list-building mechanic VUTAS's new cards/UPI checkout can implement near-identically, rather than inventing a discount structure from scratch.
  • The category is now attractive to big FMCG — a strategic-landscape fact worth knowing, not a strategy to chase. Reported interest from Emami, ITC and Britannia (even if Orkla's piece of it was denied) in a ₹2,000 Cr Farmley stake shows large incumbents now view branded healthy dry-fruit snacking as big enough to acquire into. That's a signal the overall market is validating, not a reason for VUTAS to change its curated, non-VC-backed approach — if anything it reinforces that staying lean and distinctive is more durable than trying to out-scale funded players chasing an eventual FMCG exit.
  • Watch the ownership question, not just the brand. With ~31% of Farmley's cap table reportedly for sale and large early investors seeking exit, a Farmley under new FMCG ownership could behave very differently (pricing, discounting, channel aggression) within the next year. Worth a calendar reminder to re-check this specific thread in VUTAS's next Farmley research cycle, since a completed acquisition would materially change how Farmley competes.
