Competitor Intel · Day 3

Farmley

Researched October 4, 2026

Origin story

Founded in July 2017 by Akash Sharma (CEO) and Abhishek Agarwal, two IIT graduates (Sharma from IIT Delhi, Agarwal from IIT Roorkee) who were flatmates exploring business ideas together after graduation. Rather than launching a packaged snack brand on day one, they went upstream first: the founders spent roughly four years building backend/supply-chain infrastructure — direct relationships with farming communities, farm-gate processing capability — before putting real marketing weight behind the consumer brand.

The gap they identified wasn't price or flavor — it was adulteration and traceability in a dry fruits & nuts market that is roughly 95% unorganized/loose-trade. Their answer was "full-stack" vertical integration: own the sourcing (5,000+ farmers and producers) and the processing (five farm-gate processing units near origin), rather than buying finished nuts from traders and just repackaging, the way most D2C nut brands do.

Headquartered in Noida (Delhi NCR), Farmley raised a $2M seed round in 2020 before the brand-facing push really began, meaning the commercial ramp (10x revenue growth) came largely in the ~18 months before their 2022 Series A — i.e., the backend-first bet took years to pay off, then compounded fast once it did.

Funding history: $2M seed (2020) → $6M Series A (Aug 2022, led by DSG Consumer Partners & Alkemi Growth Capital, with Omnivore, Insitor Partners and angels) → $6.7M Pre-Series B (led by BC Jindal Group, with DSG/Omnivore/Alkemi following on) → $40–42M Series C (announced May 2025, led by L Catterton, with DSG Consumer Partners and BC Jindal Group participating). Total raised to date: ~$54.7M. Valuation undisclosed at every round.

Series C mechanics are notable: co-founder Abhishek Agarwal said ~70% of the round was primary capital and ~30% was secondary — early investors Insitor Partners and Samunnati fully exited through this round, and some employees sold ESOP holdings. Stated use of funds: diversifying into healthy desserts/on-the-go snacks, capex, quick-commerce expansion, and deepening offline distribution in rural regions.

Where they sell

D2C: farmley.com  ·  Marketplaces: Amazon, Flipkart  ·  Quick commerce: Blinkit, Zepto (plus BigBasket listings) — explicitly named as a priority growth channel in the Series C funding plan.

Offline: retail presence across 20+ Indian cities as of the Series A period, with a stated target of 1,00,000+ retail outlets — a general-trade-heavy ambition rather than owned flagship stores (unlike Happilo, Farmley hasn't publicized its own branded retail stores).

International: early-stage presence in the US, Middle East and Australia, with Southeast Asia and Canada named as near-term (1–2 year) targets as of 2024.

Team: ~170 employees as of February 2024.

Strategy, positioning & product range

Farmley calls itself a "full-stack snacking brand" — the brand story leads with backward integration and traceability ("adulteration-free," farm-to-pack) rather than just taste or price. Leadership frames the brand around four pillars: trust, better-for-you nutrition, taste, and innovation.

Product strategy leans hard into innovation to escape commodity-nut pricing pressure: flavoured cashews (Thai chilli, black pepper), five flavours of makhana, trail mixes and bars, date-based bites, and novelty launches like a "world's first maida-free Makhana pasta" and a thandai nutty-milk mix. Over 100 SKUs in the catalog — the opposite end of the breadth spectrum from VUTAS's curated under-20 approach. Core target demo is urban young adults (25–35) at life-stage inflection points (new job, marriage, fitness goals) looking to "snack better."

What VUTAS can take from this

  • Backend-first, then brand — Farmley spent ~4 years on sourcing/processing infrastructure before scaling marketing, and the 10x growth came only after that foundation was in place. For VUTAS, the lesson isn't "take 4 years" but: get sourcing/QC and fulfillment genuinely solid on the new Next.js checkout before pushing paid acquisition hard — a bad first-order experience is harder to recover from than a slow start.
  • Traceability as a differentiator VUTAS can actually use. Farmley's whole brand promise is "adulteration-free, farm-to-pack." VUTAS is small enough to make sourcing transparency concrete (e.g., naming actual origin regions for anjeer/almonds on product pages) in a way a 100-SKU brand can only gesture at generically — this fits the curated, trust-forward tone the Alpine Citrus direction is going for.
  • MRP-vs-selling-price gap is a deliberate lever, not a detail. Farmley's makhana is discounted 43% off MRP on quick commerce while plain almonds sit at a modest 16% off. As VUTAS builds its own cards/UPI checkout, it should decide per-SKU discount depth intentionally (hero/trial SKUs discounted harder to drive first purchase, core nuts at a thinner, consistent margin) rather than a flat site-wide markdown.
  • Innovation SKUs carry pricing power that plain nuts don't. Date bites (~₹345/200g with flavours) and flavoured cashews/makhana price noticeably higher per gram than plain roasted nuts. VUTAS's catalog could earmark 1–2 "hero innovation" SKUs (a flavoured or bite-format product) rather than competing purely on commodity almonds/cashews pricing, where it can't out-scale Farmley or Happilo.
  • Quick commerce is now table-stakes for this category, not optional. Both Happilo and Farmley list on Blinkit/Zepto and call it a named growth priority (Farmley's Series C funds are explicitly earmarked for quick-commerce expansion). VUTAS rebuilding its D2C site shouldn't treat Blinkit/Zepto listing as a "later" channel — dry fruits are an impulse/top-up category that quick commerce captures well.
  • Revenue scale ≠ profit, even at ₹230+ Cr. Farmley posted a ₹26.49 Cr loss in FY24 despite ₹230.65 Cr revenue, only turning EBITDA-positive in FY25. This is a thin-margin category even for well-funded, vertically-integrated players — reinforces that VUTAS's curated, lower-SKU-count model (lower inventory/working-capital risk) is a reasonable hedge rather than a limitation to apologize for.
  • Don't chase 100+ SKUs to "look like" a full brand. Farmley's breadth strategy works because it's backed by five processing units and $54.7M in capital. VUTAS's under-20-SKU curation is a legitimate strategic choice at its scale, not a smaller version of what Farmley is doing — the two are different plays, and VUTAS shouldn't benchmark SKU count against brands with order-of-magnitude more funding.
