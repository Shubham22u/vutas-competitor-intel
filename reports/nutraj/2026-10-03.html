Competitor Intel · Day 2

Nutraj

Researched October 3, 2026 · Brand of VKC Nuts Private Limited

Origin story

Nutraj's own marketing dates the family business to 1926 — "VKC Nuts has been making people crazy about nuts since 1926" — trading dry fruits and spices out of Khari Baoli, Chandni Chowk, Asia's largest wholesale spice market in Old Delhi. Managing Director Gunjan Jain (family surname Jain; relatives including Shekhar Jain also appear in company records) describes the company's own self-image bluntly in one interview title: "As VKC Nuts, we are the dry fruit wallah sitting in Khari Baoli, Chandni Chowk."

The formal corporate entity, VKC Nuts Private Limited, was incorporated in 1999 (CIN registered in Jammu & Kashmir — a state that offers area-based industrial/tax incentives, which likely shaped where manufacturing was sited). The business is described as evolving from "a small walnut processing unit in Jammu" into a fully integrated nuts-and-dried-fruits conglomerate over roughly the last 15–25 years: sourcing, processing, packing, wholesaling, distributing, exporting, importing, and — much more recently — branded D2C retail under the Nutraj name.

The market gap VKC exploited wasn't a product gap so much as a structural one: it already controlled the wholesale/trading layer that most "nuts startups" have to build from scratch (sourcing relationships, mandi networks, export licenses, cold storage). Nutraj-the-brand was layered on top of an existing, decades-old B2B trading and processing business — the opposite starting point from a VC-funded D2C-first brand.

Funding history: Family-owned and self-funded through trading-business cash flow for essentially its entire history — no VC rounds through the 2010s while brands like Happilo were raising. That changed in October 2025: Belgian investment holding company Ackermans & van Haaren (AvH) agreed to invest €22 million for a 16.6% stake in VKC Nuts, co-investing alongside Venturi Fund I (€19 million) — a combined €41 million raised in the round, taking AvH's total economic interest (direct stake + its interest in Venturi Fund I) to 18.2%. Deal expected to close Q4 2025. AvH's investment director Jens Van Nieuwenborgh framed it as support for VKC's "growth strategy," calling Nutraj "one of India's most trusted healthy snack brands." The press coverage of the deal also cited India's branded dry-fruit B2C segment growing at ~22% CAGR since 2020, and value-added nut products (trail mixes, nut bars, etc.) at ~34% CAGR.

CRISIL has rated VKC Nuts SE1A — the highest rating in the SSI (small-scale industry) rating band — and the company has received APEDA awards from India's Ministry of Commerce for agri-export promotion, specifically in the walnut sector.

Where they sell

D2C: nutraj.com (consumer) and wholesale.nutraj.com (bulk/B2B for hotels, bakeries, corporate gifting)  ·  Marketplaces: Amazon (Nutraj is described as category market leader for dried fruits/nuts on Amazon India), Flipkart, Meesho  ·  Quick commerce & grocery delivery: confirmed live listings on Blinkit and Zepto, plus BigBasket.

Modern retail (offline): 500+ Reliance Retail stores across its Fresh/Smart/Mart formats, plus listings with Future Group, Bharti Retail, Walmart (cash-and-carry/wholesale), Tata Group's Trent, and Metro Cash & Carry.

Traditional trade: still active in mandis and kirana stores — the heritage Khari Baoli wholesale-trader identity the company explicitly leans on.

Owned retail — "The Nut Lounge": a premium standalone-store format selling nuts, dried fruits, seeds, berries, nut oils, nut butters and exotic spices, concentrated first in Jammu & Kashmir (its home region — an 11th store opened in Anantnag) and the NCR (Mall of India, Noida; Indirapuram Habitat Centre, Ghaziabad). Six stores were open as of 2021, with a stated target of 25 company-owned + 50 franchise stores by December 2023 and further rapid rollout promised afterward — a target that, based on available reporting, appears to have scaled more slowly than announced.

Manufacturing footprint: reporting varies by year/source — one 2022 account cites 7 plants (Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR, Mumbai, Jammu & Kashmir) with 8 more planned by 2024; a separate leadership interview describes 4 facilities (Jammu and Ghaziabad); the Oct 2025 AvH deal announcement cites 6 production facilities. The plant count has clearly grown over time and is reported inconsistently across sources, so treat the exact current number as directional, not precise. Exports reach 35+ countries. Catalog size: 200+ SKUs.

Strategy, positioning & product range

Nutraj's core positioning asset is heritage plus full vertical integration — "India's oldest dry fruits and nuts brand," built on a 1926-rooted trading identity, that now also owns processing, packing, wholesale distribution, export/import, modern retail, and D2C all at once. Gunjan Jain's own framing: "India is the fastest growing market for nuts & dried fruits," and VKC is positioned "across the entire value chain" to capture that growth rather than competing as a single-layer D2C brand.

Because of its B2B2C roots, Nutraj is also structurally a supplier to other retailers and possibly other brands' private-label lines (Reliance, Future Group, Metro Cash & Carry, exports) — the branded-D2C push (nutraj.com, Amazon leadership, quick commerce) is a comparatively recent layer added on top of a much larger, older wholesale trading business, not the company's original or primary revenue engine. The Nut Lounge retail format is the most brand-forward, premium-experience piece of the portfolio, deliberately expanding beyond plain nuts into oils, butters and exotic spices to raise basket value and in-store theatre.

The October 2025 institutional investment (AvH/Venturi) marks a visible strategic inflection — moving from a purely family-funded trading conglomerate toward professionalized growth capital, likely aimed at accelerating manufacturing capacity, international expansion, and digital/D2C investment.

What VUTAS can take from this

  • Nutraj's "moat" is a trading/wholesale business VUTAS will never have — so don't compete on scale or landed cost. Nutraj's D2C site sits on top of decades of mandi-sourcing relationships, export infrastructure, and J&K-based manufacturing incentives. VUTAS can't out-source or out-price that. The edge has to be curation, brand story, and the online buying/checkout experience itself — exactly where the Alpine Citrus rebuild should focus, rather than trying to win on raw SKU breadth or rock-bottom per-kg pricing.
  • Selective discounting, not blanket discounting, is a live strategy worth testing. Nutraj runs its plain almonds and dates at flat MRP (a trust/stability signal on staples) while discounting more differentiated combos and value-added SKUs. VUTAS could test the same split: keep hero single-origin SKUs (e.g. a flagship anjeer or a signature almond) at a consistent, undiscounted price to build trust, and reserve visible discounts for bundles/combos designed to lift average order value.
  • A heritage or origin story is a real differentiator, even against bigger players — if genuine. Nutraj leans hard on "since 1926" and the Khari Baoli identity. VUTAS doesn't have a century of trading history, but it does have a specific curation point of view (under-20-SKU, hand-picked) and the Alpine Citrus brand world — the Next.js rebuild's "About/Story" content should make that point of view explicit and concrete, the way Nutraj does with its heritage, rather than leaving it implicit.
  • Channel presence here is wide but uneven in depth — a lesson in focus, not imitation. Nutraj is live on nutraj.com, Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho, BigBasket, Blinkit, Zepto, plus 500+ Reliance stores and its own Nut Lounge chain — but its owned-store rollout (target: 25+50 stores by Dec 2023) appears to have undershot its own announced pace. For a brand VUTAS's size, the lesson is to go deep on 1–2 channels (the owned D2C site plus maybe one marketplace) and execute them well, rather than spreading thin across every channel at once the way a much larger incumbent can afford to.
  • Institutional capital just entered this category (Oct 2025, AvH/Venturi into VKC Nuts) citing 22% CAGR in branded B2C dry fruits since 2020 and 34% CAGR in value-added nut products. That's an external, investor-grade confirmation that the category VUTAS is in is genuinely growing, not stagnant — useful context for prioritizing the D2C rebuild and checkout investment now rather than treating this as a slow/legacy category.
  • Watch the B2B2C angle as a possible future lever, not a near-term distraction. A meaningful share of Nutraj's business is likely supplying other retailers/private labels rather than selling its own brand direct. VUTAS shouldn't chase this now, but it's worth remembering that wholesale/private-label supply is a real, proven revenue path in this category if the D2C brand ever needs a second growth engine.
